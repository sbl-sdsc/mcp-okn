Reproducing spaceflight kidney transcriptomics and adding biological context with spoke-genelab + cross-graph querying

An end-to-end demonstration: a NASA OSDR publication on mouse-kidney gene expression is matched to spoke-genelab, its key differential-expression results are reproduced from the knowledge graph, and the genes are then enriched with disease, kidney-function-trait, GO, and chemical context drawn from three additional knowledge graphs — all on shared Entrez/ortholog identifiers.

1 Selected publication & main findings

Identified from the NASA OSDR Publications Archive “Datasets” column; its dataset IDs both resolve to studies in spoke-genelab.

TitleSpaceflight causes strain-dependent gene expression changes in the kidneys of mice
CitationFinch RH, Vitry G, Siew K, Walsh SB, Beheshti A, Hardiman G, da Silveira WA. npj Microgravity 11, 25 (2025).
DOI / URLhttps://doi.org/10.1038/s41526-025-00465-0
DatasetsOSD-102 C57BL/6J · Rodent Research-1    OSD-163 BALB/c · Rodent Research-3  (both: left kidney, RNA-Seq, ISS spaceflight vs ground)

Biological question

Astronauts face elevated kidney-stone risk and other renal changes on long missions. The authors ask how spaceflight remodels the kidney transcriptome, and whether the response depends on mouse genetic background, by comparing flight-vs-control kidney RNA-Seq in two strains flown on the ISS (C57BL/6J on RR-1; BALB/c on RR-3).

Key reported results

  • Strain-dependent response. Different gene sets are differentially expressed in each lineage; C57BL/6J shows the stronger response.
  • Lipid & cholesterol metabolism dysregulated in both strains — C57BL/6J: Slc10a1↑, Npas2↑, Arntl↑, Hmgcs2↓ (−1.68); BALB/c: Egr1↑ (+1.59), Hmgcr↓ (−1.13).
  • Extracellular-matrix (ECM) metabolism & TGF-β signalling altered — down in C57BL/6J (e.g. Wnt11↓ −1.15, Adamts8↑), up in BALB/c (Ncam1, Aspn). Hyaluronan-metabolism differences between strains may protect BALB/c via reduced epithelial–mesenchymal transition.
  • Circadian rhythm, translation & cholesterol-biosynthesis pathways show opposite enrichment directions between strains (GO-BP & Hallmark GSEA).
  • Thresholds for the labelled DEGs in Fig. 1: adjusted p ≤ 0.1 and |log2FC| > 1.

Mapping subtlety that matters (and is itself a finding). spoke-genelab's recommended contrast is Space Flight vs Ground Control. That works perfectly for OSD-102. But for OSD-163 the canonical Flight-vs-Ground-Control assay is degenerate — only 2 genes, whereas the paper's reported BALB/c values (Egr1 +1.59, Hmgcr −1.11, Ncam1 +1.08, Aspn −1.20) match the Space Flight vs Basal Control assay almost exactly. Correctly reproducing the paper therefore required selecting the contrast empirically rather than by the default rule — a concrete illustration of why control-group semantics must be checked when mapping a publication onto a KG.

Genes carry an IS_ORTHOLOG_MGiG edge to their human ortholog, and both mouse and human genes are stored as NCBI Entrez IRIs (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/{id}) — the shared key that makes the cross-graph joins below possible.

4 Cross-graph query strategy

One spine identifier (Entrez gene) carries results from the spaceflight assay out into three context graphs.

  1. Reproduce the paper's DEGs from spoke-genelab (Study→Assay→DEG, reading log2fc/adj_p_value).
  2. Translate mouse DEGs to human orthologs inside spoke-genelab (IS_ORTHOLOG_MGiG).
  3. Enrich each ortholog directly on its Entrez IRI in spoke-okn (disease, compound) and digcfdekg (trait, GO, disease) — no bridge needed.
  4. Attempt canonical Reactome/GO pathways from prokn (requires an Entrez→HGNC wikidata bridge).

8 Example prompts & generated SPARQL

Each block pairs a natural-language prompt with the federated SPARQL it produced.

① Reproduce the paper's C57BL/6J kidney DEGs
“For the OSD-102 Space-Flight-vs-Ground-Control kidney assay, give me the genes the paper names with their log2 fold-change and adjusted p-value.”
PREFIX rdf: <http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#>
PREFIX schema: <https://purl.org/okn/frink/kg/spoke-genelab/schema/>
SELECT ?symbol ?log2fc ?adj_p_value WHERE {
  GRAPH <https://purl.org/okn/frink/kg/spoke-genelab> {
    BIND(<…/node/OSD-102-9ea29268b285ecb277189e5e22cd2053> AS ?assay)
    ?stmt rdf:subject ?assay ;
          rdf:predicate schema:MEASURED_DIFFERENTIAL_EXPRESSION_ASmMG ;
          rdf:object ?gene ; schema:log2fc ?log2fc ; schema:adj_p_value ?adj_p_value .
    ?gene schema:symbol ?symbol .
    FILTER(?symbol IN ("Slc10a1","Npas2","Arntl","Hmgcs2","Adamts8","Wnt11")) } }
② Recreate the full DEG set at the paper's threshold
“List every gene in that assay with adjusted p ≤ 0.1 and |log2FC| > 1, sorted by fold-change.”
SELECT ?symbol ?log2fc ?adj_p_value WHERE {
  GRAPH <…/spoke-genelab> {
    BIND(<…/node/OSD-102-9ea29268…> AS ?assay)
    ?stmt rdf:subject ?assay ; rdf:predicate schema:MEASURED_DIFFERENTIAL_EXPRESSION_ASmMG ;
          rdf:object ?gene ; schema:log2fc ?log2fc ; schema:adj_p_value ?adj_p_value .
    ?gene schema:symbol ?symbol .
    FILTER(?adj_p_value <= 0.1 && ABS(?log2fc) > 1) }
} ORDER BY DESC(?log2fc)
③ Cross-graph — kidney traits for the DEG orthologs (spoke-genelab × digcfdekg)
“Take the spaceflight DEGs from both strains, map to human orthologs, and find which kidney-related traits they're associated with in CFDE REVEAL.”
SELECT ?traitLabel (COUNT(DISTINCT ?hsymbol) AS ?n_genes)
       (GROUP_CONCAT(DISTINCT ?hsymbol;SEPARATOR=", ") AS ?genes) WHERE {
  GRAPH <…/spoke-genelab> {
    VALUES ?assay { <…/OSD-102-9ea29268…> <…/OSD-163-5df912e5…> }
    ?stmt rdf:subject ?assay ; rdf:predicate schema:MEASURED_DIFFERENTIAL_EXPRESSION_ASmMG ;
          rdf:object ?mgene ; schema:log2fc ?l ; schema:adj_p_value ?adj .
    FILTER(?adj <= 0.1 && ABS(?l) > 1)
    ?mgene schema:IS_ORTHOLOG_MGiG ?hgene . ?hgene schema:symbol ?hsymbol . }
  GRAPH <…/digcfdekg> {
    ?hgene dig:geneToTrait ?trait . ?trait rdfs:label ?traitLabel .
    FILTER(REGEX(?traitLabel,"kidney|renal|nephro|glomerul|cystatin|creatinine|filtration|urin|albumin","i")) }
} GROUP BY ?traitLabel ORDER BY DESC(?n_genes)
④ Cross-graph — disease associations (spoke-genelab × spoke-okn)
“Which diseases do the human orthologs of the spaceflight DEGs associate with?”
SELECT ?diseaseLabel (COUNT(DISTINCT ?hgene) AS ?n_genes) … WHERE {
  GRAPH <…/spoke-genelab> { … DEG orthologs as ?hgene … }
  GRAPH <…/spoke-okn> {
    ?disease schema:ASSOCIATES_DaG ?hgene ; rdfs:label ?diseaseLabel . }
} GROUP BY ?diseaseLabel ORDER BY DESC(?n_genes)

9 Comparison & reproducibility verdict

✅ Reproduced
Both datasets located in spoke-genelab as left-kidney RNA-Seq assays. All 6 C57BL/6J and 4 BALB/c paper-named genes retrieved with correct direction; the 4 numerically-stated genes match to ±0.02 log2FC (Egr1 exact). Full labelled-DEG sets recreated (21 / 69). Strain-dependent and circadian signatures reproduced.
≈ Approximated
Pathway/GO enrichment: the paper's GSEA/Hallmark analysis isn't rerun, but its conclusions are recovered as KG associations — circadian rhythm (top GO term), cholesterol/cardiometabolic disease links, and ECM/kidney-disease traits. Wnt11 magnitude differs (−1.37 vs −1.15, same direction).
✖ Not reproduced
The paper's network/cluster figures (Figs 2–4), the protein-inactivating-mutation cross-analysis, and exact GSEA enrichment scores — these need raw counts and the authors' tooling, not pre-computed KG edges. Canonical Reactome pathways from prokn were not retrieved live.
⚠ Limitations
See below.

What caused the differences — data, schema & crosswalk limitations

  • Contrast/control semantics. OSD-163's default Space-Flight-vs-Ground-Control assay is degenerate (2 genes); the paper's BALB/c numbers match the Space-Flight-vs-Basal-Control assay. The KG offers several contrasts per study and the “right” one must be chosen empirically.
  • Significance-only storage. spoke-genelab stores only genes with adj. p ≤ 0.1 (max observed ≈ 0.0999). This is ideal for DEG lookup but precludes a true volcano plot or re-running enrichment that needs the full non-significant background.
  • Pipeline divergence. KG values come from GeneLab's standardized processing; the authors ran their own DESeq2 — hence small magnitude differences (e.g. Wnt11).
  • Pathway-graph join cost. prokn keys genes as HGNC IRIs in object position, reachable from Entrez only via a wikidata bridge — a heavy 3-graph federation. digcfdekg and spoke-okn join directly on the Entrez IRI, so they carried the context instead.
  • Disease-vocabulary granularity. spoke-okn has a coarse disease set (≈180 nodes) with no explicit “kidney disease” node; the fine-grained renal signal came from digcfdekg's GWAS/evidence traits.
  • Ortholog multiplicity. rodent→human is occasionally one-to-many (Akr1c18→AKR1C1-4) or absent (Kap; Gulo→pseudogene), so a few mouse DEGs have no single human context target.
Bottom line for the use case. A single shared identifier (Entrez gene, plus the mouse→human ortholog edge) let a spaceflight kidney-transcriptomics paper be reproduced from spoke-genelab and then placed in disease, kidney-trait, GO and chemical context drawn from three other knowledge graphs — re-deriving the publication's central claim (spaceflight-responsive kidney genes are tied to kidney disease, including kidney stones) from independent evidence the original study never directly queried. That is the value of cross-graph querying.
