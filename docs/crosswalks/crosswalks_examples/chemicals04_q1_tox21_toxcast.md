🧠 **Assistant**

**Why the join is required.** Tox21 contributes the screening-library identity and chemical name; ToxCast contributes the assay coverage and hit calls. Neither alone can rank a Tox21-library chemical by its ToxCast activity. Tox21's chemical node IRI *is* `http://identifiers.org/cas/{cas}`, which equals ToxCast's `edam:has_identifier` object, so this is a direct CAS join with no rewrite.

**The timeout and its fix.** The activity query tested hit calls with `?hit sio:SIO_000300 ?v . FILTER(STR(?v) = "1.0")`, binding every hit-call value in ToxCast before filtering. The values are plain `xsd:string`, so the filter becomes a bound lookup — `?hit sio:SIO_000300 "1.0"` — and the query returns immediately instead of exceeding 280 seconds.

**Why this answers the question.** Coverage is nearly flat across the top of the library — 1,247 to 1,510 endpoints, a 21% spread — because ToxCast runs its full battery against the chemicals it prioritises. Coverage therefore measures *selection for testing*, not any property of the chemical. The active fraction spreads far wider, **10.5% to 40.8%, a factor of four**, and that is a property of the molecule.

**The sharpest result is PFOS against PFOA.** They are the two best-known perfluoroalkyl acids, they differ only in chain chemistry (sulfonate vs carboxylate), and ToxCast screens them almost equally hard — 1,510 and 1,396 endpoints. Yet **PFOS is active in 31.8% of its endpoints and PFOA in only 10.5%**, a threefold difference in *in vitro* promiscuity between two chemicals routinely discussed as a pair. Whatever else the two share environmentally, they do not behave alike across this assay battery.

**Triclosan and Clorophene at the top is chemically coherent** — both are chlorinated phenolic biocides, and phenols are broadly reactive across cell-based assays. That the two most promiscuous chemicals in the set are structural relatives is a sanity check on the join.

**One caution.** A high active fraction is not the same as high hazard. Broad *in vitro* activity often reflects general cytotoxicity or assay interference rather than a specific toxicological mechanism, and ToxCast hit calls carry no potency information at this level. The ranking says which chemicals perturb many assays, not which ones are dangerous at realistic exposure.

**Scope note.** Q2's six chemicals are named explicitly rather than discovered, so that table compares a chosen set; Q1 is unrestricted and does rank the library.

PREFIX rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#>
