**Complete on one side, a sliver of the other.** The join covers 100% of medical-device-kg's codes and about a third of one percent of fiokg's 3,842. That asymmetry is the point. medical-device-kg classifies its manufacturers into a deliberately narrow set of device and device-adjacent industries, while fiokg covers the whole regulated economy. The join carves the medical-device sector out of EPA's facility registry; it does not connect the two graphs broadly.

**Why the count is so small, and why that is not a weakness.** Thirteen is the size of medical-device-kg's NAICS vocabulary, not a sign of a thin join. Each code stands for an industry with hundreds to thousands of entities on each side, so the 13 shared codes relate thousands of classified device manufacturers to thousands of EPA facilities (I08-Q1 gives the per-industry counts).

**Exact, but coarse.** The code-level join is exact where the company-name route into the industry cluster (I06) is approximate. It answers a coarser question, though: same industry, rather than same firm.

fiokg's total counts distinct industry identifiers on either the primary or the secondary industry predicate. medical-device-kg's count is restricted to codes on `mdo:Manufacturer` subjects.
