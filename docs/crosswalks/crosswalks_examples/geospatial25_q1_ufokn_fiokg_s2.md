🧠 **Assistant**

**This is now an exact count, not a sample — and it is 7.8× the figure this transcript used to carry.** The 2026-06-19 run reported **2,461** shared cells from a `LIMIT 5000` slice of ufokn's 5.86M S2 property rows, documented as a lower bound because no exact query would complete. The crosswalk skeleton was repaired on 2026-09-01 and the exact answer is **19,252**. Anyone who quoted 2,461 as ufokn's overlap with fiokg understated it eightfold.

**Two fixes made it runnable, and both are reusable.**

1. **Bind the schema.org predicates instead of scanning for them.** ufokn stores the **HTTPS** schema.org form, which a bracketed `<https://schema.org/value>` cannot reach (it is canonicalized to `http`). The old query worked around that with an unbound `?pv` plus `FILTER(CONTAINS(STR(?pv),'schema.org/value'))` — a filter evaluated over every predicate of 5,858,958 property nodes. `BIND(IRI(CONCAT('https://schema.org/','value')))` reaches exactly the same triples as a bound lookup.
2. **Push `DISTINCT` before the IRI construction.** The old form built an `s2.level13.…` IRI for all 5.86M rows and de-duplicated afterwards. De-duplicating the raw id first constructs only 97,087 IRIs.

Together these turn a 90-second timeout into a sub-second query.

**Why the join is required.** ufokn maps urban flood risk to S2 cells but knows nothing about what occupies them; fiokg carries EPA-regulated facilities with their own S2 attachment but no flood model. ufokn has **no alternative join key** — its place names are generic geohash labels and its identifiers are ufokn-internal — so S2 is its only attachment surface.

**The two tables disagree, and that discrepancy is itself the finding.** Requiring `?naics rdfs:label ?industry` **silently drops the three largest categories**. A direct check confirms it: `NAICS-447190`, `NAICS-45299` and `NAICS-44711` have **no triples at all** in fiokg — they are referenced as objects of `ofPrimaryIndustry` but never described, so they carry no label. The labelled table therefore begins at rank 4 while presenting itself as a ranking, and understates the exposed population by 2,385 facilities.

That matters because the dropped codes are the environmentally significant ones. **447190 is "Other Gasoline Stations" (1,154) and 44711 "Gasoline Stations with Convenience Stores" (590)** — fuel retail with underground storage tanks, the single most flood-consequential facility class in the table, and exactly what a labels-only query would have hidden. Adding a label requirement to a query is a filter, not a formatting choice.

**Reading the rest.** The exposed set is otherwise dominated by chemical-handling industries: dry cleaners (444) with chlorinated solvents, electroplating and metal coating (283 + 215) with acid and metal baths, asphalt (287) and plastics (373) with petroleum feedstock, auto body and repair (298 + 247) with solvents and waste oil. Airports rank high partly as a **scale artefact** — one airport spans many 1 km cells — and supermarkets (270) are numerous but low-hazard. This ranks **exposure count, not risk**.

**Two shape notes.** fiokg's NAICS labels carry a trailing space (`"Other Airport Operations "`), reproduced verbatim. And in fiokg the facility reaches its S2 cell through `kwgo:sfWithin`, not `owl:sameAs` — `owl:sameAs` is carried by a different node, so pairing it with `frs:hasRecord` on the same subject returns zero rows. The count query uses the `owl:sameAs` leg (what the verified skeleton measures); the industry breakdowns use `sfWithin`, the leg that reaches a facility's EPA record.
