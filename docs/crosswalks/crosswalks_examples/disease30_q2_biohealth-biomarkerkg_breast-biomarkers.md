**Why this answers the question — each biomarker has a matching condition.** The pairing is not generic co-occurrence; each of the four biomarkers maps onto a specific condition class in the other graph:

- **Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor Measurement** ↔ **Angiogenic Switch**. VEGF is the principal driver of tumour angiogenesis, and "angiogenic switch" is the term for the transition it triggers. The measurement BiomarkerKB assays and the process BioHealthKG names are the same biology under two vocabularies.
- **platelet** ↔ **Deep Vein Thrombosis**, **Thromboembolism**, **Immune thrombocytopenic purpura**. Platelet counts are the assayed marker; cancer-associated thrombosis is the clinical consequence.
- **Plasma Tumor DNA** ↔ the metastasis cluster (bone, brain, leptomeninges, lymphatic). Circulating tumour DNA is the liquid-biopsy marker used precisely to detect disseminated disease.
- **granulocyte** ↔ the inflammatory axis running through the rest of the 634.

**A scope limitation worth stating.** Only **four** biomarkers appear here, not the 26 that BiomarkerKB records for breast cancer. The other 22 are **UniProt proteins**, and ubergraph carries no `rdfs:label` for a UniProt IRI — so requiring an ubergraph label silently restricts the result to CL cell types and NCIT measurements. That is the right scope for this question, which asked for ontology-named entities, but it is a filter on *label availability*, not on biomarker importance. The protein biomarkers are reachable through prokn instead (see the biomarkerkg × prokn crosswalk), and any count taken from this query would understate BiomarkerKB's breast-cancer panel by a factor of six.

**And a caution on the 634.** BioHealthKG's `coexists_with` is literature co-occurrence, not a causal or clinical claim, and its labels repeat the same concept at several granularities ("Metastatic Carcinoma", "Neoplasm Metastasis", "Adenocarcinoma, metastatic"). The number ranks how heavily studied a disease is; the individual pairings above are the part that carries meaning.
