**Why this answers the question.** NeST asserts only that these 20 proteins form one physical assembly under strong mutational selection; the label "BAF (SWI/SNF) complex" is the system's name, not evidence for it. The join supplies independent molecular evidence that the assembly is what NeST calls it: two mutually exclusive ATPase paralogues (SMARCA4 / SMARCA2) carry helicase and ATP-dependent chromatin-remodeler activity; the structural core (SMARCB1, SMARCC1/2, SMARCD1-3, SMARCE1, ARID1A/1B) carries chromatin-remodeler and coactivator activity; and the two bromodomain / PHD subunits (BRD7, DPF2) carry specific acetyl-lysine reader activities — H3K14ac for both, plus H3K9me2/3 and H4K16ac for DPF2. That is a functionally coherent remodelling machine reconstructed entirely from the partner graph, with no member left unannotated.

**The cancer-relevant detail the join surfaces:** three separate members — SMARCA4, SMARCB1 and BRD7 — are independently annotated with **p53 binding**. NeST's own evidence for this system is statistical (mutations recur across 13 tumour types); ProKN supplies a mechanism through which that selection could act, and it converges on p53 from three different subunits. Neither graph states this alone: NeST has the mutational signal without the mechanism, ProKN has the mechanism without the system membership or the selection.

**Coverage.** 20 of 20 members annotated — verified by a `FILTER NOT EXISTS` check for members lacking a ProKN GO function, which returned zero rows. For this system the join loses nothing.
