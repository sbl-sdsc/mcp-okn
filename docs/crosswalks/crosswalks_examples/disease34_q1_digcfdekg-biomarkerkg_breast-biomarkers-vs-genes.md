**Why the join is required.** biomarkerkg holds *measured clinical* evidence — which analytes are assayed and what their abnormality indicates. digcfdekg (CFDE REVEAL) holds *inferred genetic* evidence — which genes statistical models tie to a trait. Neither contains the other's claim, so "is what we measure in the clinic the same thing genetics points at?" is only answerable across both.

**Why this answers the question.** The two graphs describe breast cancer at **different layers, and they barely meet**. biomarkerkg's breast-cancer evidence is overwhelmingly proteomic — 26 protein analytes against only 4 gene analytes — because clinical biomarkers are things you can measure in a sample. CFDE REVEAL's evidence is entirely genetic and an order of magnitude broader: 484 inferred genes. Of biomarkerkg's 4 gene analytes, exactly **one — FOXC1 — is also among CFDE's 484**.

That single intersection is the honest headline, and it is more informative than a larger number would be. FOXC1 is a transcription factor with an established association to basal-like/triple-negative breast cancer, and it is the one place where a gene independently inferred as a genetic susceptibility driver is *also* curated as a clinically assessed marker. The other three are pure clinical-assay entities: ATOX1 (copper chaperone), AZU1 (azurocidin, a neutrophil granule protein) and MIEN1 (migration and invasion enhancer 1, in the HER2 amplicon).

**The gene/protein layer mismatch is the real finding.** A naive reading of "484 CFDE genes vs 26 biomarkers" would suggest enormous unexploited overlap. There is not — because 26 of biomarkerkg's 30 breast-cancer analytes are *proteins*, and CFDE REVEAL asserts nothing about proteins. Comparing the sets on Entrez, the only shared identifier space, reduces biomarkerkg's side to 4. Anyone wanting a genuine protein-vs-gene comparison here would need a gene→protein mapping step this crosswalk does not supply, and should not read the disease-level match (193 shared MONDO diseases in the recipe) as implying entity-level overlap.

**Identifier note.** biomarkerkg encodes gene analytes as PubChem gene IRIs, `https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/rdf/gene/GID{entrez}`, while digcfdekg uses `http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/{entrez}`. The Entrez id is extracted and re-wrapped with `IRI(CONCAT(...))`; without that rewrite the gene leg silently returns nothing.
