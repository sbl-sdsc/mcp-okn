**2. The original reported a confounded contrast as the spaceflight effect.** The hand-written filter was meant to enforce within-assay comparability, and the query then took `MAX(?sfLnfc)`. Checking the three Space-Flight-vs-Ground-Control abundance assays against the server's vetted list (the `get_valid_contrasts` tool, which computes the rule instead of trusting a hand-written filter) shows **only one of the three is a clean contrast — and it is not the one `MAX` selects**:

The two rejected assays compare **root against leaf** across the flight/ground boundary. Their differential values mix the spaceflight effect with the difference between plant compartments, so they measure compartment, not spaceflight. `MAX` picked the first of them.

**The biological conclusion survives, narrowly.** The clean contrast gives **log2FC 11.835** against the confounded 11.862 — a 0.2% difference, so Gammaproteobacteria really is strongly enriched in spaceflight roots. But that is luck, not method: the correct number and the wrong number happened to nearly coincide here, and the third assay (2.872) shows how far apart they can fall. A reader given only `MAX` has no way to know which they received.

**Why the join is required.** GeneLab measures a clade-level abundance shift but knows nothing about human disease; NDE tracks NIAID pathogens and their diseases but has no spaceflight data; and the two do not meet at species level. Only the NCBITaxon hierarchy in ubergraph can test whether NDE's pathogens sit *inside* the enriched clade.

**Why this answers the question.** All eight NIAID pathogens tested are confirmed descendants of the enriched clade, so the spaceflight signal *can* be connected to human pathogens — but only at the rank of class. Gammaproteobacteria contains most of the classic enteric pathogens (E. coli, Salmonella, Shigella, Vibrio) alongside vast numbers of harmless environmental organisms, so **clade-level enrichment is a hypothesis-generating observation, not evidence that pathogens increased**. The organisms actually enriched in the VEG-01 root samples are unidentified at species level; what the join establishes is that the enriched class is the one human enteric pathogens belong to, which is a reason to sequence deeper, not a finding about crew risk.

**Method note.** The pathogen list is supplied as `VALUES` rather than discovered, so this is a test of eight named NIAID organisms for clade membership, not an exhaustive survey of NDE.
