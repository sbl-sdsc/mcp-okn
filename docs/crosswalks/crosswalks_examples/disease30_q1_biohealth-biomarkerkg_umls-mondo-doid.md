**Why this answers the question.** The two sides line up mechanistically without being told to. BiomarkerKB's COVID-19 panel is exactly the recognised severity panel — **D-dimer**, **ferritin**, **lactate dehydrogenase**, **neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio**, **presepsin**, **creatine kinase-MB**, lymphocyte and leukocyte counts — and BioHealthKG's complications for the same disease are respiratory failure/ARDS and arterial thrombosis. **D-dimer is a fibrin-degradation product**, so the marker BiomarkerKB records and the thrombotic complication BioHealthKG predicts are two views of one coagulopathy. Neither graph states that link; the join is what puts the measurement next to the outcome it anticipates.

The same reading holds for the neutrophil markers against the respiratory outcomes: **Neutrophil Extracellular Trap** and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio sit beside acute hypoxaemic respiratory failure and ARDS, which is the neutrophil-driven lung-injury axis.

**Two cautions about the ranking.** First, complication counts track **literature volume, not clinical severity** — COVID-19's 1,154 reflects an extraordinary publication burst, and `cancer` at 995 is a high-level MONDO term absorbing everything beneath it. Second, BioHealthKG's complication labels are **near-duplicates at several granularities**: "Acute respiratory failure", "Acute respiratory insufficiency", "Acute respiratory distress" and "Acute respiratory disease" are separate nodes, so a raw count over-states distinct clinical entities. BiomarkerKB has the same issue in miniature — `leukocyte` and `Leukocyte` are two entries. Treat these numbers as a ranking of coverage, not a measure of biology.

## Literature validation

According to PubMed, COVID-19 patients are at high risk of coagulopathy and thrombosis — conditions that elevate D-dimer — with venous and arterial thromboembolism among the recognised complications. That is precisely the pairing this join produces without being given it: D-dimer on the BiomarkerKB side, arterial thrombosis and thromboembolism on the BioHealthKG side, for the same disease.
