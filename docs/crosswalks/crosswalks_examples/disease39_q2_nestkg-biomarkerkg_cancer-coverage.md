Crosswalk: nestkg × biomarkerkg on MONDO. NeST scores each protein system for mutational selection within specific tumour types and attaches the result to a MONDO cancer term; BiomarkerKG attaches biomarkers to MONDO diseases. **The one gap is the striking result.** Invasive breast carcinoma is NeST's *second best-covered* cancer — 74 implicated protein systems, more than melanoma, lung, gastric or glioblastoma — yet BiomarkerKG records **no** biomarker against that MONDO term. This is not a biological statement about breast cancer, which has among the best-established marker panels in oncology; it is a vocabulary artifact. BiomarkerKG evidently files breast-cancer markers under a different MONDO term (a parent such as "breast cancer", or a subtype) than the `invasive breast carcinoma` term NeST inherits from its TCGA cohort labels. **A direct MONDO join therefore reports a false zero here** — the case to remember when quoting this crosswalk.

**Read the pan-cancer row with care.** The 95,569 against plain "cancer" is BiomarkerKG's entire cancer-tagged corpus attaching to the generic MONDO root, which NeST uses for its pan-cancer HiSig run. It is not 95,569 markers for a specific tumour type, and it should never be reported as one.

**What the ordering shows.** Across the specific tumour types, biomarker count and NeST system count do not track each other (melanoma: 11 systems, 1,763 markers; bladder: 81 systems, 160 markers). That is expected — one counts clinical measurement effort, the other counts mutational-selection structure — and it is precisely why joining them is informative rather than redundant.
