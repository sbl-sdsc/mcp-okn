**Why this answers the question.** Fifteen CAD-relevant genes are significantly moved by spaceflight, and the pattern is coherent rather than scattered — it concentrates in **cholesterol and lipoprotein handling**, which is exactly the axis CFDE weights most heavily for CAD.

**Up:** the HDL apolipoproteins `Apoa1` and `Apoa4`, endothelial lipase `Lipg`, the sterol efflux transporters `Abcg5` and `Abcg8`, and `Mvk` (mevalonate kinase, cholesterol synthesis).

**Down:** `Pcsk9` — the LDL-receptor regulator and the target of an entire drug class — along with lipoprotein lipase `Lpl`, lysosomal acid lipase `Lipa`, and the vascular genes `Vegfa`, `Cxcl12` and `Smad3`.

The direction pattern is internally consistent: sterol *efflux and synthesis* genes up while *lipoprotein clearance* genes (`Pcsk9`, `Lpl`, `Lipa`) go down describes a shift in how cholesterol is trafficked, not a uniform up- or down-regulation. `Pcsk9` falling with the strongest lipid-gene signal here is the single most actionable row, since PCSK9 level is directly tied to circulating LDL.

**Where the evidence is thin.** Nine of the fifteen rest on a **single** clean assay. The best-supported rows are `Lipg` and `Cxcl12` (4 assays each), then `Pcsk9` (3) and the four two-assay genes. Effect sizes are moderate (|log2FC| 0.3–1.6), which is what a real tissue-level regulatory shift looks like — and notably unlike the implausible −12 values in the companion cardiomyocyte transcript.

**Two limits.** Mouse symbols are derived by capitalising the human symbol, a convention that is right for most orthologues but would silently miss any gene named differently in mouse — `Lpa` is a known case where the human gene has no true mouse orthologue. And 22 of the 37 CAD genes returned nothing: not measured, or not significant, in these 25 assays — not evidence of no effect.
