**Why this answers the question — with the significance line drawn honestly.** Three of the four reach significance: **Myl7** (myosin light chain 7, the atrial-specific regulatory light chain), **Fgf12** (an intracellular FGF that modulates cardiac sodium channels) and **Nppa** (atrial natriuretic peptide, the defining atrial secretory gene). **Erbb4 does not** — at adj. p = 0.093 it fails the conventional 0.05 threshold, and it is reported here because it was measured, not because it is a finding.

All four move in the same direction — **down** in spaceflight — and the effect sizes on the significant three are very large (log2FC −12 to −6.7, i.e. hundred- to thousand-fold). Taken at face value that is a coordinated loss of atrial cardiomyocyte identity markers, which would fit the cardiovascular-deconditioning premise.

**But the magnitudes deserve scepticism rather than a headline.** A log2FC of −12 for `Myl7` in heart tissue is not a plausible physiological change; values that extreme usually indicate near-zero counts in one arm, a normalisation artefact, or a cell-composition shift rather than per-cell regulation. Each rests on a **single assay**, so there is no replication to distinguish those explanations from a real effect. The correct reading is that these markers are strongly and consistently down in one clean heart contrast — a signal worth pursuing in the primary data, not a measured fold-change to quote.

**Coverage.** Five of the nine ProKN markers (`Ankrd1`, `Cmya5`, `Ttn`, `Ryr2`, `Myh6`) have no differential-expression record in these three assays at all. Their absence is missing measurement, not evidence of no change — which matters here, since `Myh6` and `Ttn` are the two markers whose regulation would most directly speak to cardiac deconditioning.
