The striking absence is **EGFR**. It is the single most clinically consequential gene in lung cancer — the target of the entire tyrosine-kinase-inhibitor treatment line — and biomarkerkg records it as a lung-cancer biomarker, yet CFDE REVEAL does not link it to the trait.

**That is not a data gap, and the query checks.** A third query confirms EGFR is richly represented in digcfdekg: it carries **569 distinct CFDE trait associations**. So the gene is present and heavily annotated; lung cancer simply is not one of its traits. The absence is a real property of the data, not a missing record — which is exactly the distinction a zero-row result cannot make on its own, and the reason the control query is worth running.

**Why the two sources disagree by construction.** CFDE REVEAL infers **germline susceptibility** — which inherited variation a population-genetic model associates with a trait. EGFR's role in lung cancer is overwhelmingly **somatic**: acquired activating mutations in tumour tissue, not inherited risk alleles. A germline-susceptibility resource has no reason to list it, and its absence is the expected result rather than an error.

So the honest reading of this crosswalk for lung cancer is that the two graphs are **complementary rather than corroborating**. biomarkerkg answers "what do we measure, and in what tissue," CFDE REVEAL answers "what inherited variation predisposes." Anyone who joins them expecting mutual confirmation of the same genes will find almost nothing and may wrongly conclude the crosswalk is broken; what the join actually shows is that clinical measurement and germline inference target different biology. The disease-level match is real (193 shared MONDO diseases), but it does not propagate to entity-level agreement.

**Layer mismatch, again.** 13 of biomarkerkg's 15 lung-cancer analytes are proteins, and CFDE REVEAL asserts nothing about proteins, so the comparable surface on the Entrez axis is just 2 genes. Reading "364 CFDE genes" against "13 biomarkers" as a near-miss overlap would misstate what was actually compared.
