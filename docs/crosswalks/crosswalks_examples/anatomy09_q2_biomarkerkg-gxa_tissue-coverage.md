**The two biggest sample sources in clinical practice are entirely unmatched.** Urine (740 biomarkers) and blood serum (536) have no GXA assay at all — together more biomarkers than every matched tissue except whole blood. This is not a data-quality problem in either graph; it is the boundary between the two disciplines. **You cannot RNA-seq urine or serum meaningfully**, so an expression atlas has nothing to say about the two most clinically convenient specimen types.

Also unmatched: **feces** (46, the entire microbiome-marker literature), **saliva** (10) and **semen** (35) — non-invasive specimens with no transcriptomic counterpart — plus generic UBERON parents (`bodily fluid`, `tissue`) that BiomarkerKG uses when a source is unspecified and that no assay is ever annotated to.

**Practical rule this gives you.** Before pairing a biomarker with expression data, check its sample source against this half-and-half split: solid-organ and whole-blood markers can be taken to GXA; urine, serum, saliva, feces and CSF markers cannot, and no amount of query tuning changes that.

**Method note.** The uncovered list came from a `FILTER NOT EXISTS` against GXA's `has_attribute` — which ran cheaply here because BiomarkerKG's anatomy axis is only 53 terms.
