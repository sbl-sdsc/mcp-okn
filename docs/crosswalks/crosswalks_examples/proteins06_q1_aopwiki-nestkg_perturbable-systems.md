**Why this answers the question.** The ranking is not a grab-bag — it recovers, without being told, the four axes regulatory toxicology actually models:

- **Apoptosis**, three separate systems deep: the DISC (100%), extrinsic caspase activation (88%) and the intrinsic pathway (17 of 21 members). Cell death is the canonical adverse outcome, so AOP-Wiki saturates this machinery.
- **Cell-cycle control**: the CDK4/6-cyclin complex at 100% and G1→S control at 75% — the checkpoint whose failure AOPs link to proliferative outcomes.
- **Endocrine and nuclear-receptor signalling**: androgen receptor regulation (75%) and SREBP-driven lipogenic gene expression (80%), the endocrine-disruption and steatosis AOPs.
- **Growth-factor and immune signalling**: VEGF and receptors (100%), EGFR/Her2/Her3, JAK-SHP2, interleukin production and T-cell anergy.

**What the join buys.** AOP-Wiki gives you a protein and an adverse outcome; NeST gives you the assembly that protein sits in and how strongly that assembly is mutated across tumours. Together they say something neither says alone: *a toxicant acting on this Key Event is acting inside a protein system that cancers themselves select for.* The MutL alpha/beta complex at 80% is the sharpest case — mismatch repair is both a NeST cancer system and an AOP target set, so a genotoxic AOP and a tumour-selected system are pointing at the same four proteins.

**Caveat on reading the percentages.** These are small systems (4–21 members), so a 100% is 4 of 4, not a statistical enrichment; the query ranks coverage, it does not test significance against a background. The larger systems in the table — intrinsic apoptosis (17/21) and G1→S (12/16) — carry more weight for that reason.
