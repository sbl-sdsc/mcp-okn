**Why this answers the question.** This is a real analytical panel, not a list of detections: every chemical has exactly **48** measurements, which is the signature of a fixed method run across 48 maize samples. The composition is a modern EPA-style PFAS suite — the legacy perfluoroalkyl acids (PFBS, PFDS, PFDA), the **fluorotelomer sulfonates** (4:2, 6:2, 8:2) that are current-use replacements, the **perfluorooctanesulfonamido acetic acids** (N-EtFOSAA, N-MeFOSAA) that are PFOS precursors, and the **ether-based replacement chemistries** — 4,8-dioxa-3H-perfluorononanoic acid (ADONA), perfluoro-2-methyl-3-oxahexanoic acid (GenX/HFPO-DA) and the two chlorinated polyfluoroether sulfonates (F-53B components).

That mix is the point for food-crop safety: it covers not only the banned legacy compounds but the substitutes brought in to replace them, so a crop screened on this panel is being tested for the *current* contamination profile rather than the historical one.

**Two cautions.** The equal counts mean these are **analyses performed, not positives** — the panel says what was looked for, and this query does not report concentrations or detection status. And the Arabidopsis link is a **lineage analogy, not a measurement**: SAWGraph has never measured PFAS in Arabidopsis, and GeneLab has never flown maize. The shared clade justifies treating maize uptake data as loosely informative for space-agriculture, nothing stronger.
