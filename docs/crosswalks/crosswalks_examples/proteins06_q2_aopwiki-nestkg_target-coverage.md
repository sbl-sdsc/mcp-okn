**Why the 1,376 and the 632 differ.** NeST suppresses `has_member` edges for systems above its size cutoff, and a few members have no node in the interaction network, so a protein can be a NeST protein without being retrievable as a member of a specific system. The 1,376 is the join's true size; the 632 is what you can attribute to a *named* system, and it is the number to quote when the question is about systems rather than proteins.

**How to read the 18.5%.** It is not a defect. AOP-Wiki's target set spans all of regulatory toxicology — including receptors, transporters and metabolic enzymes in tissues cancer proteomics does not emphasise — while NeST is built from a cancer protein-interaction map. An 18.5% intersection is the overlap between "proteins toxicology cares about" and "proteins under mutational selection in tumours", and that intersection reaching three quarters of NeST's systems is the striking part: toxicological perturbation is not confined to a corner of the cancer system map, it is spread across most of it.
