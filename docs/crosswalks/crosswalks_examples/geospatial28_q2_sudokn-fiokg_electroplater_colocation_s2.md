**The old access path is dead in two places.** The 2026-06-19 version constructed the site IRI (`company + '-geosite-1-instance'`) and read `s:hasGeospatialLocation` → `s:hasLatitudeValue`/`s:hasLongitudeValue`. **Join mechanics.** `spatial_bridge` fetches `?site ?lat ?lng`, computes each point's Google S2 Level-13 `CellId`, formats the KWG cell IRI, injects the `(site, cell)` pairs as `VALUES`, and joins `?cell` to FIOKG facilities whose EPA record is `ofPrimaryIndustry NAICS-332813`.

**Independently re-queried.** The second query below takes seven of those cells as a literal `VALUES` list and asks FIOKG directly which NAICS-332813 facilities they contain — confirming SILVEX, PHILLIPS PLATING, MOLITOR POLISHING, ALUMIPLATE, AAA METAL FINISHING, MIDWEST FINISHING and SPECTRUM METAL FINISHING without any injected data. **Why this answers the question, and what it is good for.** The 188 sites are the electroplaters that both a manufacturer directory and the federal regulatory registry place in the same square kilometre. Where the names also match, the pair is effectively an **entity resolution across two graphs that share no identifier** — a directory record reconciled to a regulated facility purely through geography. Where they do *not* match (73 of 188), the cell holds a different plating business nearby, which is itself meaningful: metal finishing clusters, and a 1 km cell in an industrial park routinely contains several shops.

**Read the counts as a floor.** The 500-row cap truncates the result, so 188/246/179 are lower bounds, not totals; and the co-location is proximity, not identity, unless the name check confirms it.
