## 1. Executive summary

Integrating **{{n_kgs}} OKN knowledge graphs** around one harmonised disease anchor gives a coherent picture of MS. The anchor is MONDO_0005301 = DOID:2377 = UMLS C0026769 = MeSH D009103 = ICD-10 G35. Seen through the federation, MS is a **polygenic, HLA-class-II-anchored disorder of adaptive immunity with an innate/microglial arm**. Its therapeutic landscape targets lymphocyte trafficking, lymphocyte depletion and redox, and its prevalence rises steeply with latitude. Of **{{n_genes}}** genes with any MS evidence, **{{tier_A}} reach tier A** and **{{tier_B}} tier B**. Tier A means four or more independent sources, or curated plus genetic plus one more. The top-ranked genes are **{{top10}}**.

Functional enrichment of the curated/genetic signature is overwhelmingly immune. **{{go_sig}} of {{go_tested}} GO BP terms** and **{{re_sig}} of {{re_tested}} Reactome pathways** pass FDR < 0.05. They are led by immune response, JAK–STAT, T-cell receptor and B-cell activation, and Reactome *Interferon gamma signaling* (15-fold). GO *microglial cell activation* is also enriched, which agrees with the IMSGC microglial signal (§8, Claim 2).

The **therapeutic layer** contains **{{prokn_compounds}} prokn MS-indicated compounds** ({{prokn_phase4}} at phase 4) acting on **{{prokn_targets_n}} target genes**. The requested DMT classes are mostly recorded across rdkg, prokn and wikidata. **Interferon-β, glatiramer and cladribine have no prokn drug→target edge**, so their mechanisms cannot be traced in the graph. Among the drug targets, only **{{tierA_targets}}** have tier-A genetic support. Disease-associated **expression** is noisy: across four MS-vs-control contrasts only **{{gxa_rec2}} genes recur** and **{{gxa_rec2_consistent}}** of them change in the same direction (JCHAIN, OASL up; PLAU down). By contrast, IFN-β treatment produces a robust interferon-response signature.

**Epidemiologically**, country prevalence (spoke-okn, IHME 2019; {{prev_countries}} countries) ranges from {{prev_min}} to {{prev_max}} per 100,000, highest in {{prev_top3}}. It correlates with |latitude| (Spearman ρ = **{{rho_lat}}**, p = {{p_lat}}), and the association persists after adjusting for GDP (partial ρ = {{rho_lat_gdp}}). The literature comparison (§8) supports most of the biology. It also exposes **graph errors and gaps**: biohealth lists CMV and dozens of other viruses as "causes" of MS, contradicting cohort evidence. PRMS is still a live ontology term while CIS is absent. NfL, the best-validated MS fluid biomarker, is missing from biomarkerkg.

## 3. Design & rules

The study anchors every KG on a single disease identity resolved in ubergraph. MONDO_0005301 and its subclass closure give RRMS, SPMS, PPMS, PRMS, chronic progressive, Marburg and paediatric MS. ubergraph's exact matches and cross-references then translate the anchor into each graph's own scheme: DOID for spoke-okn, prokn and wikidata; UMLS for biohealth; MONDO for rdkg, oard-kg, digcfdekg, Gene Expression Atlas, biomarkerkg and nde. Genes are integrated on **Entrez/Ensembl identifiers**, and on gene symbols only where prokn requires a label join to reach UniProt proteins. Countries are joined on **ISO3**. No join uses a study or dataset accession.

Evidence is kept in **separate categories** and never merged into one undifferentiated count:
- curated disease–gene edges (spoke-okn, rdkg);
- genetic association (digcfdekg PIGEAN, wikidata P2293);
- differential expression (Gene Expression Atlas; IFN-β treatment contrasts kept apart from disease contrasts);
- biomarker records (biomarkerkg);
- drug–target druggability (prokn);
- protein interaction (nestkg; used for ranking weight and network analysis, not counted as a source);
- clinical/EHR association (oard-kg);
- literature predication (biohealth).

Functional enrichment uses hypergeometric over-representation with BH FDR against an explicit prokn background of annotated genes. The exact signature construction, backgrounds, thresholds and scoring formula are in the specification cell of the reproducibility notebook.

## 4. Confidence tiers

| Tier | Rule (evidence categories counted over 7 gene-level sources) | Genes |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | ≥ 4 sources, or ≥ 3 sources including both a curated (spoke-okn/rdkg) and a genetic (digcfdekg/wikidata) source | {{tier_A}} |
| **B** | 3 sources, or 2 sources that are one curated + one genetic | {{tier_B}} |
| **C** | any other 2 sources | {{tier_C}} |
| **D** | a single source (mostly digcfdekg-only or expression-only) | {{tier_D}} |

The seven counted sources are spoke-okn, rdkg, digcfdekg, wikidata, biomarkerkg, Gene Expression Atlas (disease contrasts only) and prokn (drug target). Within a tier, genes are ordered by a score that adds small bonuses for PPI degree and PIGEAN score. Tier D is large by construction because the broad PIGEAN set contributes about a thousand single-source genes. Treat tier D as background, not as findings.

The federation keeps the classical clinical-course taxonomy (RRMS / SPMS / PPMS / PRMS). It has **no exact term for clinically isolated syndrome** and still treats **PRMS as a live subtype**. The 2013 revision of clinical-course definitions retired PRMS and added CIS (§8, Claim 11). rdkg's `treats` edges are attached largely to RRMS, PRMS and paediatric MS; this reflects how trial registries name their populations rather than biology. SPMS and PPMS edges come only through rdkg's grouped "progressive MS" node (ocrelizumab, ibudilast, masitinib, fluoxetine, amiloride, riluzole). Data coverage is skewed the same way: nde indexes **{{nde_MONDO_0005301}}** datasets to MS overall but only **{{nde_MONDO_0000450}}** to SPMS and **{{nde_MONDO_0000451}}** to PPMS. The progressive forms are the least represented, yet they carry the greatest unmet need.

Disease-associated expression shows **compartment-specific, poorly reproducible signals**. Only {{gxa_rec2_consistent}} of the {{gxa_rec2}} recurrent genes change in the same direction across contrasts. The rest flip between blood and CSF: AIF1, IL1RN and IL1R2 are up in RRMS blood but down in CSF leukocytes. The most coherent disease signal is local. **CSF leukocytes over-express plasma-cell and B-cell genes** (IGHM, JCHAIN, TNFRSF17/BCMA, MZB1, POU2AF1, CD38), the transcript counterpart of intrathecal immunoglobulin synthesis (§5.5). They under-express myeloid genes (SPP1, C5AR1, CH25H). Whole blood in E-MTAB-2973 carries a type-I interferon signature (IFI27, IFI44L, RSAD2, IFIT1, MX1, OAS3). This may reflect untreated disease or unrecorded IFN-β exposure, and the atlas metadata cannot tell these apart. The **IFN-β treatment contrasts reproduce a canonical ISG response** (IFI27 +7.3 log2FC, CCL2, CCL8, CXCL10, SERPING1, RSAD2), concordant with published pharmacogenomic studies (§8, Claim 14). The monocyte contrast is dominated by Y-chromosome genes and is uninformative after correction. Progressive MS is barely represented: the SPMS cortex contrast (E-GEOD-32645) returned no DE genes at threshold.

![Therapeutic landscape](figures/fig4_drug_landscape.png)

Columns: rdkg `treats` (q17, q18); prokn MS indication (q21/q22, DOID_2377/2378/0050783 and MeSH D009103 nodes); prokn drug→target (q22, UniProt mapped to gene); wikidata P2176 (q39). **{{dmt_all4}} of {{dmt_rows}}** DMT rows are present in all four layers (fingolimod, alemtuzumab, fumarates, teriflunomide, mitoxantrone, natalizumab). Every class has at least one KG record. Mechanisms trace cleanly for the S1P modulators (S1PR1/S1PR5 → lymphocyte sequestration), anti-CD20 antibodies (MS4A1), natalizumab (ITGA4/ITGB1, VLA-4), alemtuzumab (CD52), fumarates (KEAP1 → NRF2 redox), teriflunomide (DHODH) and BTK inhibitors (BTK). **{{dmt_target_missing}} have no prokn drug→target edge**, so their mechanism is invisible to a graph traversal. Notably, most approved targets carry **only tier B–D genetic support**: S1PR1 and MS4A1 are B, ITGA4/KEAP1/BTK are C, DHODH is D. The genetically best-supported druggable genes (**{{tierA_targets}}**) are targeted mainly by agents that never reached MS approval (daclizumab, withdrawn; secukinumab, ustekinumab and briakinumab at phase 2). Genetic risk and therapeutic leverage therefore only partly overlap.

**Repurposing.** {{repurpose_n}} prokn MS-trial compounds act on tier A–C genes without being approved MS DMTs (workbook sheet *Repurposing*). The genetically strongest are the IL-12/23 and IL-17A antibodies and belimumab (TNFSF13B/BAFF). Because their recorded MS phase is ≤ 2, these are candidates whose genetic rationale has **not** translated, not untested opportunities. Less explored targets with genetic support and an existing drug are **VDR** (cholecalciferol; tier A), **CYP24A1** (tier A) and **TYK2** (tier A, a JAK-family kinase with approved inhibitors in other diseases; no TYK2 compound appears in the MS-indicated set). The remyelination candidates in prokn (opicinumab/LINGO1, elezanumab/RGMA, clemastine/HRH1) target genes with **no MS genetic support** in any KG, so repair biology is reachable only through the drug layer.

The curated MS phenotype is classic and neurological: spasticity, weakness, incoordination, paresthesia, diplopia, bladder dysfunction, emotional lability, depressivity and CNS demyelination. Only **{{pheno_rdkg_in_oard}} of {{pheno_rdkg}}** curated phenotypes reach the oard-kg co-occurrence threshold (spasticity, muscle weakness, depression). The EHR layer is dominated by **non-specific or comorbid terms** (obesity, copper and iron indices, cardiac and paediatric feeding terms), which indicates cohort mixing and reflects the observational nature of oard-kg. Among the oard-kg neurological terms, hypertonia, spasticity, vertigo, gait disturbance, fatigue, tremor and seizure agree with clinical MS.

**Biomarker ranking** (by cross-KG support):
1. **MRI and lumbar puncture**, both recorded by wikidata. CSF immunoglobulin increase is supported by biomarkerkg (CSF, serum, plasma) and by the CSF plasma-cell transcript signature (§5.3).
2. **Genetic risk markers**: {{bm_risk}} SNPs plus the tier-A HLA class II genes.
3. **Pharmacodynamic markers** of approved drugs: S1PR1/S1PR5 and SPHK1/2 for S1P modulators; the IFN-response genes for IFN-β.
4. **Cytokine analytes** IFNG and IL17A, which are also tier-A genes.

Neurofilament light chain, the most clinically validated progression and response marker, **is absent** from biomarkerkg (§8, Claim 9). So are oligoclonal bands as a named entity. No KG offers a progression-specific biomarker.

Both families were **run** (GO BP and Reactome). GO molecular function and cellular component were **skipped** because the question concerns processes and pathways, and MF/CC add little at this signature size. Disease/trait gene-set enrichment against digcfdekg was **skipped** because digcfdekg supplies part of the foreground, so the test would be circular. The signal is **adaptive immunity first**. T-cell receptor signalling and activation (×15), JAK–STAT signalling (×19) and B-cell activation/proliferation (×15–21) lead GO. *Interferon gamma signaling* (×15), IL-4/IL-13, IL-10, IL-2, PD-1 co-inhibition and the MHC-II/ZAP-70 synapse pathways lead Reactome. Innate terms follow: NK-cell activation, LPS response, IL-1β production, NLRP3 inflammasome and **microglial cell activation** (×12, FDR ≈ 2×10⁻⁶). No term for myelination, oligodendrocyte differentiation, astrocyte function, axon degeneration, mitochondria, oxidative stress or complement reached the testable k threshold. The CNS-intrinsic arm of MS is **not represented** in the genetic/curated signature. It is reachable only through drugs (KEAP1, LINGO1, RGMA) and the CSF transcriptome. Enrichment is descriptive and inherits the composition of the signature.

## 7. Discussion

Taken together, the axes describe MS as a disease whose **inherited risk sits in antigen presentation and lymphocyte activation**. In order:
1. HLA class II, IL2RA, IL7R, CD58 and CD6 carry the inherited risk.
2. That risk is expressed through **IFN-γ- and IL-12/23-centred cytokine networks** (enrichment and the PPI core).
3. It becomes visible clinically as **intrathecal B-cell/plasma-cell activity**: the CSF transcriptome, the immunoglobulin biomarkers and the success of anti-CD20 therapy.
4. It is modulated geographically by latitude-linked exposures. Vitamin D has genetic (VDR, CYP24A1) and Mendelian-randomisation support, while EBV is supported by the literature but only weakly encoded in the graphs.

The therapeutic layer mostly acts **downstream of the genetic signal**, on trafficking, depletion and redox. Directly targeting the most strongly supported cytokine genes (IL-12/23, IL-17A, IL-2Rα) has not produced approved drugs.

**Implications and testable predictions:**
- **Genetic follow-up.** Tier-A genes without drugs but with tractable biology are candidates for target validation: TYK2 (approved inhibitors exist in other indications), CD40/CD40L co-stimulation, and MERTK/NLRP3 on the innate arm.
- **PPI-neighbourhood curation.** High-degree tier-D genes adjacent to tier-A genes (CD2, LCP2, ITGB2, VAV1, CD28/CD27 by PIGEAN) should be prioritised for curation. The prediction is that fine-mapping will place several of them among causal genes.
- **Progressive MS.** The scarcity of progressive-MS data across nde, the Gene Expression Atlas and rdkg is the most actionable gap. The KG view predicts that progressive-stage signatures will be enriched for microglial and BTK-pathway genes rather than T-cell genes, consistent with the progression-specific benefit of tolebrutinib in nrSPMS (§8, Claim 13).
- **Federation fixes.** Retire or annotate PRMS, add CIS, add NfL and OCBs to biomarkerkg, and add effect sizes or negation to biohealth predications. These would remove the main sources of KG–literature conflict.

## 10. Summary of findings & limitations

**Findings.** Integrating {{n_kgs}} OKN knowledge graphs on a harmonised MS anchor yields {{tier_A}} tier-A and {{tier_B}} tier-B genes. The core is HLA class II, IL2RA/IL7R co-stimulation and IFN-γ/IL-12/IL-17 cytokine signalling, confirmed by GO/Reactome enrichment ({{go_sig}} and {{re_sig}} significant terms) and a cytokine-centred PPI network. Disease expression is compartment-specific: CSF leukocytes carry a plasma-cell signature, and IFN-β treatment a canonical ISG response. The therapeutic landscape ({{prokn_compounds}} indicated compounds; {{prokn_targets_n}} targets) mostly acts on trafficking, depletion and redox rather than on the top genetic genes. Prevalence follows latitude (ρ = {{rho_lat}}) independently of income. The literature comparison supports the adaptive-immune core and identifies specific KG errors (viral `causes` edges, PRMS) and gaps (NfL, CIS, progressive MS, CNS repair biology, ncRNA, gene–environment interaction).

**Limitations.**
1. All gene–disease, phenotype and risk-factor relationships are associative (curated, GWAS-derived, text-mined or EHR co-occurrence); none supports causal inference.
2. Query results were capped at 1,000 rows. digcfdekg was recovered with tail queries ({{dig_genes}} of 1,059 genes), and about 13 PIGEAN ≥ 3 genes may be missing from the enrichment foreground.
3. Enrichment used prokn's direct (non-propagated) GO annotations, tested only terms with k ≥ 5 (GO) or k ≥ 6 (Reactome), and lost 125 of 345 signature symbols that did not resolve to prokn genes (a label-join limitation).
4. The tier rule is an analyst choice. The broad PIGEAN set inflates tier D, and genes lacking curation (e.g. CD28, CD27) are under-ranked.
5. Gene Expression Atlas contrasts are few, heterogeneous (blood, monocytes, CSF), partly sex-confounded (E-GEOD-77598) and almost absent for progressive MS. IFN-β exposure in the disease cohorts is not recorded.
6. oard-kg EHR co-occurrences include strong cohort noise (obesity, paediatric and cardiac terms); biohealth predications lack effect sizes and negation.
7. Prevalence is a single IHME 2019 snapshot; **no incidence data, no sub-national (e.g. US state) prevalence and no SDoH edges** for MS exist in the queried KGs. WHO GHE deaths are rounded and available for only {{n_mort}} countries, and the spoke-okn per-100k mortality field is mislabeled and was not used.
8. GDP per capita and country boundaries come from Natural Earth, an external non-KG covariate. Country-level correlations are ecological.
9. Ontology mismatches (no CIS term, PRMS retained, SPMS/PPMS missing MeSH/NCIT) limit subtype-resolved analysis.
10. The literature comparison used PubMed abstracts and metadata only; no claim was verified against full text.
11. Two logged exploratory-stage queries (q15, q34) were superseded because of a SPARQL `BIND` scoping error, and q23 by q24. They are excluded from the reproducibility record and from all results.
